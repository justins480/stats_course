About this Tool

This interactive explorer is designed to visually demonstrate dispersion. Each generated sample contains values constrained between -10 and +10 so the scale remains easy to interpret, but the mean is allowed to vary naturally from sample to sample. Use the individual X and Y displays to see how far each observation falls from its sample mean, then compare those distances with the reported standard deviations. The scatterplot shows how the same observations appear when the two variables are viewed together.

Exploration Tips

Generate New Sample: Creates a completely new random sample with values constrained between -10 and +10. Compare how the mean and standard deviation change from one sample to the next.
Add More Points: Adds observations to the current sample while keeping all values between -10 and +10. Watch how the sample mean and standard deviation respond as the sample grows.
Clear All Data: Removes all observations so you can begin a new exploration.

Understanding Mean & Standard Deviation

Mean: The arithmetic average of the observations. It identifies the center of the current sample and is allowed to vary naturally as the data change.
Standard Deviation: Describes how far observations tend to fall from the mean. A larger standard deviation indicates greater dispersion.
Connect the Number to the Picture: Compare the reported standard deviation with how far the individual observations fall from the sample mean in the X and Y displays. The mean may change when you generate a new sample or add observations. Each line shows an observation's deviation from that sample mean. Longer lines indicate greater dispersion; shorter lines indicate less dispersion. All generated values are constrained between -10 and +10 to keep the scale easy to interpret.

Reading Dispersion in the Scatterplot

Each point represents one observation measured on both variables. The red dot marks the current pair of sample means (mean X, mean Y). Because the means are allowed to vary naturally, this point may move from sample to sample. The faint lines connect each observation back to that center so you can visualize its distance from the mean.

When points stay close to the center, the data show less dispersion. When points extend farther away, the data show greater dispersion. Compare these distances with the X and Y standard deviations above: larger standard deviations should correspond to a wider spread of points along that axis. All X and Y values are constrained between -10 and +10 for a consistent teaching scale.

Mean and Standard Deviation

Mean: The average value, shown as a vertical line. It represents the center of your data distribution.
Standard Deviation (SD): Measures spread. In a normal distribution, about 68% of data falls within ±1 SD of the mean.
Larger SD = More Spread: When points are scattered far from the mean, SD is large. When points cluster near the mean, SD is small.
